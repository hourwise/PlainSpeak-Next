# Review of the pilot scheme

The pilot ran for eleven months across four sites. Attendance was lower than
forecast at every site, and the reasons differed enough that no single
explanation covers all four.

Nevertheless, the scheme met its primary objective. The number of referrals
completed within the target window rose from 61% to 78%, and the improvement
held in each of the last five months rather than appearing only at the end.

Nevertheless, the cost per completed referral remained above the figure set out
in the original business case. Staff time accounted for most of the difference,
and the gap narrowed only slightly over the year.

Site A recorded the strongest performance. It also had the most experienced
team, which the evaluation could not separate from the effect of the scheme
itself.

Nevertheless, the two smaller sites reported improvements of a similar size.
That is the finding the panel considered most encouraging, since neither site
had the staffing advantage.

Nevertheless, none of the sites reached the 85% target. Three of the four came
within seven percentage points of it.

Nevertheless, the panel recommends continuing the scheme for a further year.
The evidence for a permanent change is not yet strong enough, and a second year
would produce enough data to settle the question either way.

Nevertheless, the funding decision rests with the board, which meets in March.
