# What your local library offers

Libraries lend books. They also do a lot of other things that people do not
always know about. This page explains what is available and how to use it.

Everything on this page is free unless it says otherwise.

## Joining

Anyone who lives, works or studies in the county can join. Joining is free.

You can join online or at any branch. To join at a branch, bring something with
your name and address on it. A bank statement, a utility bill or a driving
licence all work. If you do not have any of these, ask staff. They can usually
find another way.

Children can join from birth. A parent or carer signs the form.

You get a card. Use the same card at every branch in the county.

## Borrowing

You can borrow up to 20 items at once. Books, audiobooks, music and DVDs all
count towards this.

Books are lent for three weeks. You can renew them twice, unless someone else has
reserved the item. Renew online, by phone or in a branch.

There is no charge for late books. There used to be. There is not now.

DVDs are lent for one week and cost £1.50 each.

If you lose an item, tell staff. You will be asked to pay what it costs to
replace it.

## Reserving

You can reserve any item in the county's stock. Reserving is free.

Order it online or ask staff. It will be sent to the branch you choose. You will
get an email or a text when it arrives. Collect it within a week.

If the county does not have the item, staff can request it from another library
service. This costs £4 and takes longer.

## Computers and internet

Every branch has computers you can use. Using them is free.

Book a computer in advance or ask at the desk. Sessions last one hour. You can
have more time if nobody is waiting.

All branches have free wifi. You do not need a library card to use the wifi.

Printing costs 10p a page in black and white and 40p in colour. Scanning is free.

## Help with going online

Some people find computers difficult. Staff can help.

Most branches run a session where a volunteer sits with you and helps with one
task. This might be filling in a form, setting up an email address or using video
calls. Sessions last about 45 minutes.

Ask at the desk or phone your branch. You do not need to book far ahead.

## Reading at home

You can borrow ebooks and audiobooks without going to a branch. Download the
Libby app and sign in with your library card number.

The county buys a set number of copies of each ebook, so popular titles have a
waiting list, the same as printed books.

Large print books are on the shelves in most branches. Ask staff if you cannot
find what you want.

If you cannot get to a library, the home library service brings books to you. A
volunteer visits once every three weeks. Phone the main library number to ask
about this.

## Children and families

Every branch has a children's area. Children can borrow books on their own card.

Story sessions run in term time. They are aimed at children under five and last
about half an hour. No booking is needed.

The summer reading challenge runs from July to September. Children read six books
and collect stickers. It is free to take part.

Homework help is available at four branches after school. Check the website for
days and times.

## Study space

All branches have tables you can work at. Nobody will ask why you are there.

Three branches have quiet study rooms that you can book for free. Two have rooms
that groups can hire. Group rooms cost £12 an hour.

## Local history

The central library holds records for the whole county. This includes:

- parish registers
- census records
- local newspapers on microfilm
- maps going back to 1780
- photographs

You do not need to book to use the local history room. You do need to book to use
the microfilm readers, because there are only four.

Staff can help you get started. They cannot do research for you, but they will
show you where to look.

## Events

Branches run author talks, reading groups, craft sessions and language exchanges.
Most are free. Some ask for a small charge to cover materials.

The full list is on the website and on the noticeboard in each branch.

## Opening hours

Hours are different at each branch. Check the website or phone before you travel.

The central library is open seven days a week. Most other branches close on one
weekday and on Sundays. Three of the smallest branches are open on alternate
weeks.

## If something is wrong

Tell staff if you have a problem. Most things can be sorted out at the desk.

If you are not happy with the answer, ask for the branch manager. If you are
still not happy, you can complain to the council. The website explains how.

## Accessible services

Tell staff if you need help using the library. Nobody will mind being asked.

Every branch has at least one step-free entrance. Six branches have a lift to the
upper floor. The website lists the access arrangements for each building.

The following are available at all branches:

- large print books
- audiobooks on CD and to download
- a magnifier at the enquiry desk
- adjustable chairs at the computers

Three branches have a reading machine that scans a page and reads it aloud. Ask
staff to show you how it works.

You can also borrow items on someone else's behalf. Bring their card and a note
from them.

## Donating books

Branches accept donations of books in good condition. Bring them to the desk.

Staff decide what goes on the shelves and what is sold to raise money. Not
everything can be added to the stock. This is about space rather than quality.

The library cannot accept encyclopaedias, textbooks more than five years old,
magazines or books that are damp or damaged.

## Suggesting a purchase

You can suggest a book the library does not have. Fill in the form on the website
or ask at the desk.

Staff read every suggestion. They cannot buy everything, but a title that several
people ask for is likely to be bought.

You will be told what was decided.
