# How to appeal a decision about your claim

You can appeal if you think a decision about your claim is wrong. This page
explains what to do and how long it takes.

Read the whole page before you start. You will need some information from your
decision letter.

## Before you appeal

You must ask for a mandatory reconsideration first. This means asking us to look
at the decision again. You cannot appeal until we have done this.

Ask for a mandatory reconsideration within one month of the date on your decision
letter. The date is at the top of the letter.

We will write to you with the result. This is called a mandatory reconsideration
notice. Keep this letter. You will need it to appeal.

If more than one month has passed, you can still ask. Tell us why you could not
ask sooner. We will decide whether to accept a late request.

## When you can appeal

You can appeal after you get your mandatory reconsideration notice.

You can appeal if:

- you think the decision is wrong
- you think we did not have all the facts
- you have new evidence

You cannot appeal because you disagree with the law. An appeal looks at whether
the decision was applied correctly.

## How to appeal

Appeal to an independent tribunal. The tribunal is not part of our department.
The people who decide your appeal did not make the original decision.

You can appeal online or by post.

To appeal online, use the appeal service on GOV.UK. You will need:

- your National Insurance number
- your mandatory reconsideration notice
- details of why you think the decision is wrong

To appeal by post, fill in form SSCS1. You can get this form from GOV.UK or from
your local Jobcentre Plus. Send the form and a copy of your mandatory
reconsideration notice to the address on the form.

Keep a copy of everything you send.

## Time limits

You must appeal within one month of the date on your mandatory reconsideration
notice.

If you miss this time limit, you can still send your appeal. Explain why it is
late. A judge will decide whether to accept it. You cannot appeal at all after 13
months.

## What happens next

The tribunal will send you a letter to confirm they have your appeal. This
usually takes about two weeks.

We will then send the tribunal our response. This explains why we made the
decision. The tribunal will send you a copy. Read it carefully.

You can send more evidence at any time before your hearing. Send it to the
tribunal, not to us.

## Your hearing

Most appeals have a hearing. You can choose whether to attend.

You are more likely to succeed if you attend. Most people who attend their
hearing find it less formal than they expected.

A hearing usually takes 30 to 60 minutes. There will be a judge. For some
benefits there will also be a doctor or a disability expert.

You can:

- speak for yourself
- bring someone to speak for you
- bring someone for support

You can ask for the hearing to happen without you. Write to the tribunal and say
this. The tribunal will decide your appeal using the papers.

## Getting help

You do not have to pay for help with an appeal.

Citizens Advice can help you fill in the forms and can sometimes come to the
hearing with you. Contact your local Citizens Advice.

Some law centres and welfare rights services also help with appeals. Your local
council can tell you what is available in your area.

## Costs

Appealing is free. You do not pay a fee.

You can claim travel costs for going to your hearing. Ask the tribunal how to do
this. Keep your tickets or receipts.

You cannot usually claim the cost of a solicitor.

## The decision

The tribunal will usually tell you the decision on the day of the hearing.

You will also get the decision in writing. This is called a decision notice. It
usually arrives within a few days.

If the tribunal agrees with you, we will change the decision about your claim. If
you are owed money, we will pay it. This usually takes up to four weeks.

If the tribunal does not agree with you, the original decision stays the same.

## If you think the tribunal made a mistake

You can ask for a written statement of reasons. This explains how the tribunal
reached its decision. Ask within one month of the decision notice.

You can only appeal against a tribunal decision if you think the tribunal made a
mistake about the law. You cannot appeal because you disagree with the outcome.

You must ask the tribunal for permission first. Ask within one month of the
written statement of reasons.

## While you wait

Keep claiming any benefits you are getting now. An appeal does not stop your
other claims.

Tell us if your circumstances change. This includes changes to your income, your
health or who lives with you. Report changes even while your appeal is waiting.

Tell the tribunal if you move house or change your phone number. If they cannot
contact you, they may decide your appeal without you.

## If your circumstances are urgent

Tell the tribunal if you need your appeal heard quickly. Reasons might include
serious illness, or a risk of losing your home.

Write to the tribunal and explain the situation. Send any evidence, such as a
letter from your doctor or from your landlord. A judge will decide whether to
list your appeal sooner.

Asking for an urgent hearing does not affect the outcome of your appeal.

## If you need support to take part

Tell the tribunal what you need. Ask when you send your appeal, or as soon as you
know.

The tribunal can arrange:

- an interpreter, including British Sign Language
- a hearing room you can reach in a wheelchair
- a hearing by video or telephone
- extra breaks during the hearing
- papers in large print or another format

There is no charge for any of this. Ask early, because some arrangements take
time to book.

## Keeping track

Write down the date you sent each thing and keep a copy. Note who you spoke to
and when.

The tribunal will give your appeal a reference number. Use this number every time
you contact them.

If you have not heard anything for eight weeks, phone the tribunal and ask for an
update.
