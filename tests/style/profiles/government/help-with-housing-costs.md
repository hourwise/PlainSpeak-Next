# Help with housing costs

You may be able to get help paying your rent. This page explains what help is
available, who can get it, and how to apply.

The help you can get depends on your circumstances and on the benefits you
already receive.

## Housing costs in Universal Credit

Most people who need help with rent get it as part of Universal Credit. This is
called the housing costs element.

You can get the housing costs element if you:

- pay rent for the home you live in
- have savings and capital below £16,000
- are aged 18 or over, or 16 or 17 in some circumstances

You cannot get the housing costs element if you own the home you live in. There
is separate support for mortgage interest.

If you already claim Universal Credit, report your housing costs in your online
account. You will be asked for your tenancy agreement and the amount of rent you
pay.

If you do not yet claim Universal Credit, apply for Universal Credit and include
your housing costs in that application.

## How much you get

The amount depends on where you live, how many bedrooms you need, and whether you
rent from a private landlord, a council or a housing association.

If you rent privately, the amount is capped at the local housing allowance rate
for your area. This rate is set once a year. It does not always cover the full
rent.

If you rent from a council or a housing association, the amount is based on your
actual rent. It may be reduced if you have more bedrooms than the rules allow.

Some costs are never covered. These include:

- water charges
- heating, lighting and hot water
- meals
- charges for cleaning shared areas, in some tenancies

Your rent statement should show which charges are eligible.

## How many bedrooms you are allowed

The rules allow one bedroom for each of the following:

- an adult couple
- a person aged 16 or over
- two children of the same sex under 16
- two children under 10, whatever their sex
- a child who cannot share because of a disability
- a carer who stays overnight regularly, if you or your partner need overnight care

If you rent from a council or housing association and have more bedrooms than
this, your housing costs element is reduced. The reduction is 14% for one extra
bedroom and 25% for two or more.

## Housing Benefit

Housing Benefit has been replaced by Universal Credit for most people. You can
still claim Housing Benefit if you:

- have reached State Pension age, or
- live in supported, sheltered or temporary accommodation

Apply to your local council. Your council will ask for proof of your identity,
your income, your savings and your rent.

## Discretionary Housing Payments

A Discretionary Housing Payment is extra help from your council. It is separate
from Universal Credit and Housing Benefit.

You can apply if you already get help with your rent and still cannot cover your
housing costs. Councils often use these payments to help with:

- a shortfall between your rent and the local housing allowance rate
- a reduction because of extra bedrooms
- a deposit or rent in advance for a new home
- rent arrears that put your tenancy at risk

Each council decides its own rules and has a fixed budget. Payments are usually
made for a set period rather than indefinitely.

Apply through your council's website. If your application is refused, ask the
council to look at it again and explain why you need the payment.

## If you are behind with your rent

Tell your landlord as soon as possible. A landlord who knows about a problem
early has more options than one who finds out through arrears.

Ask your council's housing options team for advice. They can help whether or not
you are at risk of losing your home, and asking early does not affect what help
you can get later.

You can also ask for part of your Universal Credit to be paid directly to your
landlord. This is called a managed payment. Your landlord can request this too.
It can be arranged whether or not you are in arrears.

## If you have to leave your home

Contact your council straight away if you are homeless or expect to become
homeless within 8 weeks.

Your council must give you advice. It must also assess whether it has a duty to
help you find somewhere to live. This assessment is free.

Do not wait for a court date. The council can usually do more to help before a
possession order is made.

## Changes you must report

Report changes in your circumstances as soon as they happen. Changes include:

- a change to your rent
- moving home
- someone moving in or out
- a change in your income or savings
- a child turning 16 or leaving education
- going into hospital or a care home for an extended period
- leaving the country for more than a month

Report changes through your Universal Credit account, or to your council if you
claim Housing Benefit.

If you do not report a change, you may be paid too much. You will normally have
to pay this money back.

## If you disagree with a decision

You can ask for a decision to be looked at again. This is called a mandatory
reconsideration for Universal Credit, or a revision for Housing Benefit.

Ask within one month of the date on your decision letter. Explain why you think
the decision is wrong and send any evidence you have.

If you still disagree after that, you can appeal to an independent tribunal.

## Getting advice

Advice is free and confidential.

Citizens Advice can help you check what you are entitled to and can help with
applications and appeals. Shelter gives housing advice, including an emergency
helpline. Your council's housing options team can advise on homelessness and
tenancy problems.

You do not need to pay anyone to make a claim on your behalf.
