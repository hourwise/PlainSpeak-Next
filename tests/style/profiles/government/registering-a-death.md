# Registering a death

You must register a death within 5 days in England and Wales, and within 8 days
in Scotland. The 5 days include weekends and bank holidays.

This page explains who can register a death, what you need to bring, and what you
will be given.

## Before you register

A doctor will give a medical certificate showing the cause of death. In most
cases the doctor sends this directly to the register office.

The registrar cannot register the death until they have this certificate. If you
have not been told that it has been sent, phone the register office before you
travel.

Sometimes a death is referred to the coroner. This happens when the cause is
unknown, when the death was sudden or unexplained, or when it happened during an
operation. The coroner may order a post-mortem or open an inquest.

If the coroner is involved, you cannot register the death until the coroner has
finished. The coroner's office will tell you what to do and will keep you
informed. You can still arrange a funeral in most cases, and the coroner can
issue an interim certificate so that you can deal with the estate.

## Where to register

Register the death at a register office in the area where the person died.

You can go to a different register office if travelling is difficult. The details
will then be passed to the correct office. This takes longer, and you will not be
given the documents on the day.

Most register offices ask you to book an appointment. Book online or phone the
office. Appointments usually last about 30 minutes.

## Who can register a death

The following people can register a death, in this order of preference:

- a relative
- someone who was present at the death
- an administrator from the hospital, if the person died in hospital
- the person arranging the funeral, but not the funeral director

Only one person needs to attend. That person is called the informant.

## What to take with you

Take the following if you have them:

- the person's NHS medical card
- their birth certificate
- their marriage or civil partnership certificate
- proof of their address, such as a utility bill
- their passport or driving licence

Do not delay the appointment if you cannot find these. They are helpful but not
required. The registrar will ask you questions instead.

You will need to tell the registrar:

- the person's full name at the time of death, and any names previously used
- their date and place of birth
- their last address
- their occupation
- the full name, date of birth and occupation of a surviving spouse or civil
  partner
- whether they were getting a State Pension or any other benefit

Take your own identification as well.

## What you will be given

The registrar will give you:

- a certificate for burial or cremation, known as the green form, which you give
  to the funeral director
- a certificate of registration of death, form BD8, for benefit purposes

You can also buy death certificates. These are the copies that banks, insurers,
pension providers and probate services will ask for.

Each certificate costs £12.50 at the time of registration. Buying them later
costs the same but takes longer to arrange.

Think about how many you need before your appointment. Most families need between
4 and 10. Organisations often want to see an original certificate rather than a
photocopy, and several may need one at the same time.

## Tell Us Once

Tell Us Once lets you report a death to most government organisations in one
step.

The registrar will give you a Tell Us Once reference number and explain how to
use it. You can use the service online or by phone.

Tell Us Once contacts:

- HM Revenue and Customs
- the Department for Work and Pensions
- the Passport Office
- the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency
- the local council, for council tax, blue badges, library cards and electoral
  registration
- some public sector pension schemes

Tell Us Once does not contact banks, insurers, private pension providers,
utilities or landlords. You must contact these yourself.

Use the service within 28 days.

## If the death happened abroad

Register the death according to the rules of the country where the person died.
You will be given a local death certificate.

You can also register the death with the British authorities. This is optional.
It means a record is held in the UK and you can order certificates here later.

Contact the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office for advice on bringing a
person's body home, which needs the local death certificate and permission from
the coroner in England, Wales or Northern Ireland.

## Costs and help with costs

Registering a death is free. You pay only for certificates.

You may be able to get help with funeral costs if you receive certain benefits.
This is called a Funeral Expenses Payment. Apply within 6 months of the funeral.

If the person had no family and no funds, the local council or the hospital will
arrange a public health funeral.

## Getting support

Register office staff deal with this every day and are used to helping people who
are upset or unsure. Ask them to repeat anything you did not follow.

Bereavement support is available from Cruse Bereavement Support and from many
local services. Your GP can also refer you.

## After registering

You will need to deal with the person's affairs. This can take several months.

Contact each organisation the person dealt with. This usually includes banks,
building societies, insurers, pension providers, utility companies, the landlord
or mortgage lender, and any subscriptions.

Most organisations have a bereavement team. Phoning that team directly is quicker
than going through a general number.

Ask each organisation what it needs. Some accept a certified copy of the death
certificate. Some want to see the original and will return it.

## Dealing with the estate

You may need probate before you can deal with the person's money and property.
Probate is the legal right to do this.

You may not need probate if the estate is small, or if everything was owned
jointly and passes automatically to the surviving owner. Each bank sets its own
limit for what it will release without probate.

Apply for probate online or by post. There is a fee if the estate is over £5,000.

If there is a will, the executors named in it apply. If there is no will, the
closest relative applies for letters of administration instead.

## Benefits and pensions

Report the death to the Department for Work and Pensions as soon as you can. Tell
Us Once does this for you.

Payments stop from the date of death. Money paid after that date usually has to
be repaid.

You may be able to claim Bereavement Support Payment if your husband, wife or
civil partner died and you are under State Pension age. Claim within 3 months to
get the full amount. You can claim up to 21 months later, but you will get less.
