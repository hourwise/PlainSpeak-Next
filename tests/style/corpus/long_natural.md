# Notes on running a small team

Hiring is the part everyone gets wrong first.

The instinct is to look for someone who can do the job today. That works, once.
The second time you hire that way you notice the team has stopped changing shape,
because everyone you brought in was chosen against the same snapshot of what the
work looked like eighteen months ago.

Ask instead what the person will be doing in a year.

Onboarding matters more than most people admit, and it is almost always
underfunded. A new colleague spends their first fortnight forming a model of how
decisions get made here — who to ask, what is written down, which of the written
things are actually true. If nobody helps them build that model deliberately,
they build it from whatever they happen to bump into, which is usually the most
frustrated person in the room.

Write things down. Not everything: the things you find yourself explaining twice.

One-to-ones are not status meetings. If the whole half hour is spent on what
happened last week, something has gone wrong, because that information was
available in a dozen other places. The half hour is for the things that do not
appear anywhere else — what is bothering them, what they want to be doing, what
you have not noticed.

Cancel them rarely. Cancelling one is a small thing. Cancelling three in a row
says something you did not mean to say.

On feedback: give it close to the event, in private, and be specific about the
behaviour rather than the person. That advice is in every management book ever
written and it is still the thing most often got wrong, usually because the
conversation was delayed until it had become a big one.

Finally, and this is the part nobody tells you: most of the job is noticing.
Noticing that someone has gone quiet. Noticing that two people are solving the
same problem separately. Noticing that a piece of work has been "nearly done"
for three weeks. None of that shows up in a tracker, and all of it is your job.
