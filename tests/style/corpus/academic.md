# Reading time and comprehension

The relationship between reading speed and comprehension has been studied for
most of a century, and the results are less tidy than the popular literature
suggests. Faster readers do not, in general, understand less; nor do slower
readers reliably understand more.

What appears to matter is the match between the reader's prior knowledge and the
demands the text makes. A specialist reading within their field processes
technical vocabulary as single units, which is not available to a reader
encountering the same terms for the first time.

This complicates the readability formulas that remain in wide institutional use.
Those formulas count syllables and sentence length, both of which are proxies
for processing difficulty rather than measures of it. A short sentence composed
entirely of unfamiliar terminology will score well and read badly.

None of this means the formulas are useless. It means they measure the surface
of a text, and the surface is correlated with, but not identical to, the
difficulty a particular reader will experience.
